# Security e-Drift — locked voice card

Source: grilling session with James (Aug 2026). **Confirmed.**  
Gate: if copy is not in this voice and vision, it does not go live.

## Who it’s for

Non-technical people who need to protect themselves. No-nonsense. Jargon reduced or free.

When a technical term must appear, lead with the protective action in plain words, then the name and abbreviation:

> an additional step to guard your account by applying multi-factor authentication (mfa)

## Who’s speaking

James / Security e-Drift — a real person teaching habits, not a brand deck.  
If it doesn’t sound like him, it doesn’t publish.

## Core move (at the moment of risk)

Sharp only when the reader is about to act (click, transfer, download, share a code). Elsewhere: calm.

Pattern:

1. **Stop!**
2. **Pause!**
3. Before you click the link, answer:
   - Is it urgent?
   - Do you know who it’s from?
   - Is it really from who it’s from?
4. If the answer is **not sure** or **no** — it is highly likely not from the person they think, and likely to catch them out.

## After reading

Empower or teach. The reader should turn it on themselves, e.g.:

> Ah — if I use a password that is something I know and only five characters, it is easy to lose my account. What is the password or passcode on my bank? Is it strong enough?

They should leave knowing what to check next time, without needing someone in the room.

## Texture

- Personality and local colour are welcome (**C**).
- Stories (Jim’s shop / river, etc.) can carry more colour (**D**).
- How-tos stay clearer but still sound like a person, not a template.

## Shape

- **Open:** name the risk in plain English, then the habit. Use a self-check question when that *is* the lesson (passwords, MFA, backups).
- **Length:** depends on topic and post intention — as long as the truth needs; no padding.
- **“I”:** mix of light lived-in lines and short real examples when they teach something.

## Kill on sight

- Soft corporate: “In today’s digital landscape…”
- Fake warmth: “You’ve got this!” / “Remember, you’re not alone”
- Influencer urgency: “Most people get this wrong…”
- Neat essay endings: “Final thoughts” / “In conclusion”
- Over-tidy triplets and buzzword stacks
- Scare-selling, fake certainty, American influencer tone
- Checklist-bot endings (“Remember this…”, “A habit to keep…”)
- Anything that wouldn’t survive a kitchen conversation

## Lugton / services

Educational posts stay free of mid-article soft-sell.  
A soft footer or About nod is allowed in concept only — keep it light and rare.

## Parked (future)

Interactive posts that ask about the reader’s setup (or let them click through checks) and adapt the message. Not required for current static posts; openings may still plant the self-check question in prose.

## How agents should use this

1. Draft or revise against this card first.
2. Prefer the stop → pause → three questions pattern at decision points.
3. Prefer self-check endings over summary boxes.
4. Deploy only after a human (James) would recognise it as his.
