## Mission

Cut through the noise and explain AI, cybersecurity and tech in a way folk can trust — steady, honest, and rooted in real-world graft. No hype. No scare stories. Just straight answers people can rely on.

## Audience

Technically curious readers, IT pros, security teams and business leaders who want things explaining properly — clear, calm and without any faff.

(Homes, freelancers and small teams still read here; write so a sharp non-specialist can follow, and a practitioner won’t feel talked down to.)

## Core values

- **Accuracy over speed** — better to be right than rushed.
- **Practical advice over theory** — what actually works, not what looks clever on paper.
- **Explain why, not just what** — folk deserve the reasoning, not just the headline.
- **Build trust, not fear** — cybersecurity’s serious enough without adding drama.
- **Human judgement remains essential** — tools help, but people make the calls.

## What this tone achieves

- A calm authority that feels lived-in, not performative.
- A trustworthy voice — the “I’ll tell you straight” approach Yorkshire is known for.
- A reader-friendly style that cuts jargon unless it genuinely earns its keep.
- A sense of practical wisdom rather than tech-industry theatrics.

## Craft rules (how to write it)

### Jargon

Use a technical term when it earns its keep. Lead with the plain meaning, then the name:

> an additional step to guard your account by applying multi-factor authentication (mfa)

### At the moment of risk

Sharp only when the reader is about to act (click, transfer, download, share a code, let a bot send). Elsewhere: calm.

Pattern that has earned its keep:

1. **Stop!**
2. **Pause!**
3. Before you click the link, answer:
   - Is it urgent?
   - Do you know who it’s from?
   - Is it really from who it’s from?
4. If **not sure** or **no** — treat it as a catch.

### After reading

Leave them able to decide without you in the room — a self-check or the *why*, not a summary box or pep talk.

### Shape

- Open with the real issue in plain English, then the habit or reasoning.
- Length follows topic and intention — no padding.
- Lived-in “I’ve seen…” lines when they teach something; no performative guru voice.
- Stories (Jim’s shop / river) may carry more colour; how-tos stay straighter but still human.

### Kill on sight

- Soft corporate: “In today’s digital landscape…”
- Fake warmth: “You’ve got this!” / “Remember, you’re not alone”
- Influencer urgency / scare-selling / fake certainty
- Neat essay endings: “Final thoughts” / “In conclusion”
- Checklist-bot endings (“Remember this…”, “A habit to keep…”)
- Tech-industry theatrics, hype, or anything that wouldn’t survive “I’ll tell you straight”

### Lugton / services

Educational posts stay free of mid-article soft-sell. Soft footer / About only, and lightly.

### Parked (future)

Interactive self-checks that adapt to the reader’s setup.

## How agents should use this

1. Draft or revise against **mission, audience, values, and tone** first.
2. Use Stop → Pause → three questions at decision points; explain *why* the habit works.
3. Prefer practical self-checks over summary boxes.
4. Deploy only if James would recognise it as his — steady, honest, no faff.
