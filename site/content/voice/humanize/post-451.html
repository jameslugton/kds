No fear this time — excitement. The email congratulates you by name. You have been selected for an “exclusive reward”: a brand-new HP laptop from a well-known retailer. Bright “Claim Reward Now” button. Your email address listed as “account information.”

There is no laptop and no prize. Just a form or a “delivery fee” page waiting for your details — and excitement does the same job urgency does elsewhere: it gets you moving before you verify.

What it claimed

  • You won — selected for an exclusive reward
  • HP laptop from a familiar retailer
  • Personal touch: your name and email in the message
  • Footer disclaimer that the advertiser “does not manage the subscription” — common on mass-sent scam campaigns

Why it is bait

  • Too good to be true — retailers do not give laptops to random email addresses
  • Fake personalisation — your name is inserted; everything else is generic
  • Sender mismatch — display name says the retailer; the real address does not
  • Pushy button — “Claim Reward Now” before you have time to think
  • No competition entry — did you actually enter anything? Usually no.

Click through and you get a fake claim form (personal details) or a small “delivery fee” page (card number). Sometimes both. The goal is fraud or selling your data. The reward is the bait.

Stop. Pause. Ask three questions.

Before you claim anything:

  • Is it urgent?
  • Do you know who it is from?
  • Is it really from who it is from?

Prize scams lean on hurry — claim before it expires. Excitement is the urgency. You did not enter a draw you cannot name? The sender address does not match the shop? Not sure? Likely a catch. Do not click. Do not pay a “delivery fee.”

What to do instead

  • Delete the email. Do not reply.
  • Check the real sender address — not just the display name.
  • If you genuinely entered a competition, log into the retailer’s site yourself — typed address or app — and look for notifications there.

Real companies do not hide prizes behind vague emails and mismatched domains. Already entered card details? Tell your bank and see what to do after a miss.

A self-check

When an email says you have won, what is your first move — check whether you entered anything, or picture the prize? Excitement gets ahead of logic faster than fear. Which would you do if the same message landed tomorrow?
