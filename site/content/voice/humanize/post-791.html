Shadow IT used to mean the USB stick and the unapproved app. Shadow AI is the same pattern in a new coat: a free chatbot in a personal browser tab, a clever browser extension, a “quick” site that rewrites text — none of it on the family or work approved list.

The risk is not curiosity. It is invisibility. Tools you do not admit you are using still see what you paste, and they still create accounts, cookies, and habits outside any shared safety net.

If nobody knows the tool exists, nobody can help when it goes wrong — and you may not remember what you gave it.

1. What shadow AI looks like at home and at work

  • Personal AI sites used for work emails or customer notes
  • Browser extensions that “summarise any page”
  • Phone apps that want contacts, photos, or microphone access for “writing help”
  • Shared PCs with half a dozen forgotten AI logins still open

2. Why it rhymes with shadow IT

Unapproved software, mystery cloud drives, and random USB tools create the same blind spot: data leaves the path you thought it was on. AI simply makes that path feel helpful and modern, so we lower our guard.

3. A calmer way to stay visible

  • Prefer known tools with clear privacy settings — and actually open those settings.
  • Do not paste customer, payroll, or health details into free novelty sites.
  • Review browser extensions quarterly; remove what you do not recognise.
  • If you use AI for work, ask what the official option is instead of inventing a parallel one.

Name the tool out loud. If you would feel awkward telling a partner, IT friend, or manager which site you used, that is a signal to slow down.
