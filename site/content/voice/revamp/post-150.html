Most account takeovers and odd social posts do not start with a dramatic hack. They start with ordinary gaps — weak or reused passwords, overdue updates, or one rushed click.

Cyber hygiene is the small set of habits that closes those gaps before they pile up. Not just for tech people — for anyone who uses email, banking, or a phone.

What it means

Everyday security practices that keep your devices, accounts, and data safer. Like washing hands — small, repeated actions that stop bigger problems later.

The must-have habits

  • Passwords and mfa — unique logins for important accounts; an extra phone check on email first
  • Updates — phones, laptops, browsers, routers; auto-update where you can
  • Phishing pause — verify senders; treat urgency as a warning sign
  • Safer browsing — look for the padlock on payment pages; be careful on public Wi‑Fi
  • Backups — important files copied somewhere the malware cannot reach
  • Social media awareness — limit what strangers can learn about your routines and contacts

At the moment of risk

Stop! Pause! Is it urgent? Do you know who it is from? Is it really from who it says it is? When an email feels wrong, that pause is cyber hygiene in action — not paranoia.

A self-check

Pick three habits from the list above. Which one have you actually kept this month — and which one keeps slipping? Do the slipping one today. One small job beats a perfect plan you never start.
