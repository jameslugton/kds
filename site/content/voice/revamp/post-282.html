Some malware is noisy: locked screens, pop-ups, a machine that suddenly crawls. Covert malware is quieter. It tries to blend in, collect what it can, and stay longer than a smash-and-grab.

You may not get a dramatic warning. You might get odd battery drain, unfamiliar browser helpers, or connections you never meant to allow. On a small shop PC, that can mean customer details leaving through a door nobody is watching.

What helps in ordinary places

  • Keep the operating system and browser updated
  • Use built-in protection; skip random “optimiser” downloads
  • Limit admin rights for day-to-day use
  • Be stubborn about unexpected installers and email attachments

If a device starts behaving oddly

Disconnect it from the network and get a clean look at what changed. Do not keep logging into banking on it “just to finish something.”

Stop! Pause! Is it urgent? Do you know who it is from? Is it really from who it says it is? A rushed download that “just worked” is worth a second look before you trust that device again.

A self-check

When did your laptop or phone last feel slower, hotter, or stranger after you installed something? If you cannot name what changed — run a scan, review recent installs, and stop using that device for passwords until you know.
