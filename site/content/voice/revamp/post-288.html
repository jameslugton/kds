Cybersecurity often sounds like specialist tools. Day to day, a short habit usually matters more: a few minutes to glance at devices, inbox, and accounts — the way you’d check the door is locked.

Here’s a simple 10-minute routine. Keep what fits. Skip what doesn’t.

1. Device glance (2 minutes)

Quick look — not a deep dive:

  • Unexpected pop-ups?
  • Antivirus still running?
  • Updates waiting?
  • Device unusually slow or hot?

You’re noticing, not fixing everything at once. Early notice is the point.

2. Inbox sweep (3 minutes)

Scan for:

  • Login or password-reset alerts you didn’t trigger
  • “Urgent” messages that don’t feel right
  • Attachments from unknown senders
  • Supplier or colleague emails that look slightly off

Stop! Pause! Before you click: Is it urgent? Do you know who it’s from? Is it really from them? Not sure — don’t click. Check another channel.

3. One small account job (3–5 minutes)

Pick one:

  • Update one old or short password
  • Add an extra step to guard one account with multi-factor authentication (mfa)
  • Remove an unused login or app connection
  • Check recent logins on email or banking

One small job beats a perfect plan you never start.

A self-check

Ah — if you use a password that is something you know and only five characters long, it is easy to lose that account. What is the password or passcode on your bank app? Is it strong enough? If the honest answer is no, change that one today.
