Bright red banner. “FINAL NOTICE TODAY.” Your antivirus subscription failed to renew, it says — device unprotected, personal data at risk until you update payment. A version of this lands in shared inboxes every few weeks.

It plays on a simple worry: is my machine actually protected? The email is not from your antivirus vendor. It wants your card.

What it claimed

  • Subscription failed to renew — payment issue
  • Protection deactivated; identity theft and data exposure warned
  • Update payment details to stay protected

Why it is bait

  • Heavy urgency — “FINAL NOTICE TODAY,” deactivation imminent
  • Sender mismatch — company name on screen, unrelated address behind it
  • Vague account talk — no device name, no subscription tier, no last-four on the card
  • Fear stacking — identity theft, digital threats, data exposure all in one message
  • Real companies rarely shout like this — renewal problems show in your account first

Click the link and you get a fake payment portal: card details, billing address, sometimes date of birth. Not antivirus renewal — card harvesting.

Stop. Pause. Ask three questions.

Stop!
Pause!

Before you update payment:

  • Is it urgent?
  • Do you know who it is from?
  • Is it really from who it is from?

Red banners and “final notice” are designed to skip your thinking. Do you even have a paid subscription with this company? Does the sender address match? Not sure? Likely a catch. Do not click. Do not enter card details.

What to do instead

  • Delete the message.
  • Open your antivirus app or log into the vendor’s website directly — typed address, not the email link.
  • Check renewal status and payment method inside your real account.

Genuine billing problems appear there. Already submitted card details? Tell your bank and follow what to do after a miss.

A self-check

Do you know when your antivirus actually renews — or would an unexpected “final notice” catch you on a busy afternoon? Most people do not track subscription dates. That gap is what this email exploits. Where would you check first next time — the app, or the link?
