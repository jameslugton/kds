Scams rarely look like scams. They arrive as a friendly message, a missed delivery text, a “payment failed” email, or a WhatsApp from someone who sounds like family. The channel changes. The pressure doesn’t.

You don’t need to be technical to protect yourself. You need a habit you can use when you’re tired and in a hurry.

Stop. Pause. Ask three questions.

Before you click the link — or reply, or transfer, or share a code — answer these:

  • Is it urgent?
  • Do you know who it’s from?
  • Is it really from who it’s from?

If you answer not sure or no, it is highly likely not from the person you think — and likely to catch you out. Don’t click. Don’t reply. Don’t pay. Check through a route you trust (the official app, a number you already have, or the person themselves on a known channel).

Where scams show up

Email — display name looks official; real address doesn’t. Vague “final notice” language. Links that don’t match the label. Hover or long-press before you tap.

Phone — unexpected “security checks”, pressure to install software, or asks to move money. If the voice sounds like family but the ask is odd and urgent, hang up and call them back on a number you already know. Voice cloning is cheap now; urgency is still the tell.

WhatsApp / texts — “I’ve changed my number”, gift cards, delivery fees, account suspensions. New numbers claiming to be family are a classic. Same three questions.

Social / Marketplace — new profiles, “move off the platform”, too-good offers, fake payment screenshots. If payment has to leave the usual route, stop.

Doorstep — unclear ID, pressure to decide now, refusal to leave paperwork. You’re allowed to say no and close the door.

A quick self-check

Think about the last odd message in your phone. Did you feel rushed? Did you know the sender for certain — or only the name on the screen? If you weren’t sure, what would you do differently next time?
