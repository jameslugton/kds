Automation feels like momentum. You set a rule, approve a plan, or tell a helper to “finish the rest.” Then it keeps going — polite, tireless, and indifferent to whether the first five rows were already wrong.

The quiet risk is not that software is evil. It is that it is obedient.

Three good rows do not prove the four-hundredth is fine — but three bad ones are a clear stop sign.

Watch the first batch

If you start a bulk send, a sheet cleanup, or a job where the bot can act without you, look at the first handful of results before you walk away. Confidence is not the same as correctness.

Put a human pause on the sharp edges

Anything public, financial, or customer-facing should need a person to confirm. Draft-only first. Live action second. Money and mailbox identity are not places for “run for all” curiosity.

Stop! Pause! Before you approve a bulk send or payment run: who will see the first results, and how do you stop it if they look wrong? If you cannot answer both, do not let it run unsupervised.

Know how you stop it

Before you enable a connector, write down — even roughly — how you would pause the automation, revoke the app, and sign out sessions. If you cannot answer that in five minutes, you are not ready to let it run while you make tea.

That is how much damage if it goes wrong — the blast radius — stays small enough to recover from.

A self-check

Name one automation or “run for all” job you use. When did you last check the first few outputs before walking away? If you cannot remember, check the next batch before it finishes — and stop if anything looks odd, even if the progress bar says keep going.
